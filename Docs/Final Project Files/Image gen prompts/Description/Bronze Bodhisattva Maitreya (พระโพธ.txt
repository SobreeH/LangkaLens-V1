Bronze Bodhisattva Maitreya (พระโพธิสัตว์เมตไตรยะสำริด)

- Period / Dating: 12th-13th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 7th-8th centuries CE)
- Material: Cast bronze (lost-wax technique)
- Dimensions: Height: 16.2 cm
- Discovery Location: Ban Lan Khwai (Moo 5, Kolam Sub-district), Yarang District, Pattani Province
- Current Repository: Bangkok National Museum (Phra Nakhon National Museum)

Curatorial Description:
Cast in bronze using the traditional lost-wax (cire perdue) technique, this statuette portrays Bodhisattva Maitreya, the future Buddha who resides in Tusita Heaven awaiting descent to the earthly realm following Gautama Buddha. The deity is sculpted in a graceful, slender tribhanga (triple-bend) stance that imparts a fluid, naturalistic rhythm to the torso and hips. Although both arms are broken off and missing below the shoulders, the preserved body contours retain fine anatomical balance.

The Bodhisattva's hair is swept up into a tall, tiered ascetic crown of matted locks (jatamukuta), adorned at the front center with a miniature stupa—the primary iconographic symbol identifying Maitreya. A sacred cord (yajnopavita) drapes diagonally over the left shoulder across the chest, while a lower skirt-cloth (sabong) wraps around the hips, fastened securely at the waist with a tied sash (ratprakot).

Stylistically, the modeling of the tiered hairstyle and the clinging drapery of the lower garment exhibits strong affinities with South Indian and Sri Lankan sculptural traditions. Discovered in the Yarang archaeological sphere, this statuette serves as significant material evidence of the sophisticated metallurgy, maritime cross-cultural exchange, and Buddhist devotional culture that flourished within the ancient kingdom of Langkasuka.
