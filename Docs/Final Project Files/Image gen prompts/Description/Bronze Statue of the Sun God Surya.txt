Bronze Statue of the Sun God Surya (พระสุริยะสำริด)

- Period / Dating: 15th-16th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 10th-11th centuries CE)
- Material: Cast bronze
- Dimensions: Height: 20.30 cm | Width: 10.30 cm
- Discovery Location: Ban Kuwing, Sakho Bon Sub-district, Mayo District, Pattani Province (approx. 7 km east of Yarang Ancient City)
- Current Repository: Unknown / Unrecorded

Curatorial Description:
Cast in bronze, this intricate sculptural triad depicts Surya, the Hindu Sun God and supreme deity of the Saura sect. The composition features Surya standing in a rigid upright frontal pose (samabhanga) atop a lotus pedestal mounted over his celestial single-wheeled chariot. The chariot is drawn by seven galloping horses—with the central steed emerging directly from the wheel's hub—and is steered by the divine dawn charioteer, Aruna.

Surya is flanked by his celestial retinue and personifications of dawn:
- Pingala & Goddess Usha (Right): The attendant Pingala stands in a graceful dvibhanga posture holding a writing implement and an inkpot, fronted by a relief image of the dawn goddess Usha, the dispeller of darkness.
- Danda & Goddess Pratyusha (Left): The attendant Danda stands in a dvibhanga pose holding a ritual attribute (possibly a conch), fronted by the partially preserved form of Goddess Pratyusha.
- Regalia & Attributes: Surya wears a tall conical jewel crown (Kiritamukuta), a bare upper torso, fitted trousers (sanapphlao), and knee-high riding boots. Both hands are raised at hip level to hold stemmed lotus bouquets that rise symmetrically to the level of his head.
- Aureole & Royal Canopy: The grouping is framed by an openwork arched halo (prabhavali) bordered with bead moulding and stylized leaf-like flame rays, surmounted at the peak by a royal tiered parasol (chattr) draped with fluttering banners and garlands.

Artistic & Cultural Significance:
Stylistically, this sculpture exhibits strong affinities with the 10th-11th century CE Pala-period art of Northeast India (Bengal). Discovered in the immediate vicinity of Yarang Ancient City, the artifact provides key archaeological evidence of the active practice of the Saura tradition and highlights the extensive maritime trade and religious networks connecting the Indian subcontinent with the ancient peninsular kingdom of Langkasuka.
