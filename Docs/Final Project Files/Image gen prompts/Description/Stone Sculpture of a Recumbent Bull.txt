Stone Sculpture of a Recumbent Bull / Nandi (ประติมากรรมรูปวัวศิลา)

- Period / Dating: 11th-12th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 6th-7th centuries CE)
- Material: Carved sandstone (Hin Sai)
- Dimensions: Height: 52 cm | Length: 66 cm | Width: 29.5 cm
- Discovery Location: Excavated from Ancient Monument No. 8, Ban Jaleh, Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province
- Current Repository: Songkhla National Museum

Curatorial Description:
Discovered beside an interior brick pedestal within an 8x8 meter pillared sanctuary hall at Ban Jaleh Monument No. 8, this monolithic sandstone sculpture portrays a humped zebu bull (tha non mop) resting in a tranquil, recumbent posture with its front legs folded beneath its chest. The sculpture features smooth, minimalist surfaces devoid of ornamental harnesses or jewelry, emphasizing the bull's naturalistic anatomy, upright ears, short horns, and a pronounced dorsal hump rising above the shoulders.

In ancient South and Southeast Asian art, the bull holds dual religious significance:
- Hindu / Shaivite Iconography: The bull represents Nandi (Kho Nonthi), the sacred vehicle (vahana) and divine gatekeeper of Lord Shiva. Sculptures of Nandi are traditionally placed near sanctuary entrances facing inward toward the central linga.
- Buddhist Cosmology & Lineage: The bull embodies Gotama (referencing Shakyamuni Buddha's lineage name) and serves as the Guardian Animal of the Western Direction. This directional motif draws directly from early Indian traditions—such as Emperor Ashoka’s pillar capitals featuring the elephant (East), horse (South), bull (West), and lion (North)—as well as the carved semicircular moonstones (Sandakada Pahana) of Sri Lanka's Anuradhapura period (4th-16th centuries BE).

The discovery of sandstone chisel debitage alongside a bronze Avalokiteshvara head near the sanctuary base indicates that this monolithic sculpture was carved locally, standing as physical evidence of the syncretic Hindu-Buddhist devotional culture that thrived within the ancient maritime entrepot of Langkasuka.
