**Terracotta Miniature Stupa with Inverted-Bowl Dome**

* **Dating / Period:** 12th–14th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 7th–9th centuries CE)


* **Material:** Fine-grained baked clay (Terracotta)


* **Discovery Location:** Ban Wat / Ban Jaleh archaeological complex, Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province


* **Description:** A free-standing votive stupa (*Sathup Din Phao*) crafted as a sacred Buddhist offering. The structure rises from a circular stepped pedestal with a distinct recessed waist (*thong mai*). The central bell dome is sculpted into an inverted-bowl form (*ong rakhang song o khwam*). Rising directly from the dome is an integrated conical spire formed by concentric horizontal ring ridges (*plong chanai*), which tapers steadily toward a pointed lotus-bud finial (*bua tum*). Votive objects of this type were buried within brick sanctuary foundations to generate religious merit.

**Architectural Terracotta Stupa Base with Kudū Niches**

* **Dating / Period:** 12th–14th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 7th–9th centuries CE)


* **Dimensions:** Diameter: 11.50 cm | Height: 16.50 cm


* **Material:** Baked clay with applied relief and red mineral pigment traces


* **Discovery Location:** Excavated at Ancient Monument Mound No. 3, Ban Jaleh, Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province


* **Current Repository:** Fine Arts Department Office No. 11, Songkhla


* **Description:** A cylindrical architectural stupa base featuring decorative details created through the applied clay technique (*pan-pae*). The lower rim is bordered by a tooth/gear frieze supporting alternating blind horseshoe-arch niches (*kudu*) flanked by slender rounded pilasters. Adjacent panels display blind rectangular windows fitted with vertical wooden-style balustrade bars (*lak krong mai*). The upper register is crowned with false projecting roof rafters (*khue*), four-petaled floral rosettes, and an upward-lotus moulding that originally supported a dome and tiered spire. This artifact illustrates early Dvaravati–Gupta architectural styles replicated on a miniature scale.

**Terracotta Stupa with Water-Pot Dome and 7-Tiered Chatra-valee Spire**

* **Dating / Period:** 12th–14th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 7th–9th centuries CE)


* **Material:** Fine-grained baked earthenware


* **Discovery Location:** Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province


* **Current Repository:** Fine Arts Department Office No. 11, Songkhla


* **Description:** A modular votive stupa featuring a high stepped base that supports a bulbous, water-pot shaped bell dome (*ong rakhang song mo nam*). Its upper superstructure is composed of a separate conical spire featuring seven concentric umbrella discs (*chatra-valee*) stacked along a central shaft in decreasing size, terminating in an elongated lotus-bud finial (*bua tum*). The seven tiered umbrellas represent the ascending celestial realms of Buddhist cosmology, embodying the devotional ceramic arts of the ancient maritime kingdom of Langkasuka.
