**Architectural Terracotta Stupa Base with Kudū Niches**

* **Dating / Period:** 12th–14th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 7th–9th centuries CE)


* **Dimensions:** Diameter: 11.50 cm | Height: 16.50 cm


* **Material:** Baked clay with applied relief and red mineral pigment traces


* **Discovery Location:** Excavated at Ancient Monument Mound No. 3, Ban Jaleh, Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province


* **Current Repository:** Fine Arts Department Office No. 11, Songkhla


* **Description:** A cylindrical architectural stupa base featuring decorative details created through the applied clay technique (*pan-pae*). The lower rim is bordered by a tooth/gear frieze supporting alternating blind horseshoe-arch niches (*kudu*) flanked by slender rounded pilasters. Adjacent panels display blind rectangular windows fitted with vertical wooden-style balustrade bars (*lak krong mai*). The upper register is crowned with false projecting roof rafters (*khue*), four-petaled floral rosettes, and an upward-lotus moulding that originally supported a dome and tiered spire. This artifact illustrates early Dvaravati–Gupta architectural styles replicated on a miniature scale.
