Stone Dharmachakra / Wheel of the Law (ธรรมจักรศิลา)

- Period / Dating: 11th-12th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 6th-7th centuries CE)
- Material: Carved sandstone (Hin Sai)
- Dimensions: Height: 33.5 cm | Wheel Diameter: 22.5 cm | Hub Diameter: 6 cm
- Discovery Location: Ban Wat Ancient Monument Mound No. 25 (corresponding to Mound No. 6 today), Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province
- Current Repository: Princess Galyani Vadhana Institute of Cultural Studies, Prince of Songkla University, Pattani Campus

Curatorial Description:
Carved from dense sandstone, this monolithic Dharmachakra (Wheel of the Law) represents an early aniconic tradition in Buddhist art established prior to the depiction of the Buddha in anthropomorphic form. The wheel symbolizes the First Sermon (Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, or the "Turning of the Wheel of the Dhamma") delivered by the Buddha at the Deer Park in Isipatana near Varanasi.

The sculpture features a circular wheel with 8 openwork spokes radiating outward from a smooth, unadorned circular hub. Structurally, the wheel is mounted directly atop a solid rectangular stone block, which rests on a rounded, flattened circular base pedestal.

This piece shares notable stylistic and iconographic parallels with early stone Dharmachakras found at Oc Eo in Vietnam and the Khao Phra Narai (Khao Siwichai) archaeological site in Phunphin District, Surat Thani Province. Its presence at Yarang provides vital archaeological evidence of the region's active participation in early trans-peninsular trade networks and its significance as a major Buddhist center within maritime Southeast Asia during the pre-Dvaravati and early Dvaravati eras.
