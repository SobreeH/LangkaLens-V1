Tiered Umbrella Spire of a Miniature Terracotta Stupa (ชิ้นส่วนสถูปดินเผาส่วนฉัตรวลี)

  

    Period / Dating: 12th–14th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 7th–9th centuries CE)  

    Material: Fine-grained baked earthenware / Terracotta (Din Phao Nuea La-iat)  

    Dimensions: Total Height: 46 cm | Base Diameter (Bottom Plate): 16.7 cm  

    Provenance / Discovery Site: Ban Jaleh Monument Complex, Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province (Surveyed by Anan Wattananikorn, B.E. 2505–2516 / 1962–1973)  

    Current Repository: Princess Galyani Vadhana Institute of Cultural Studies, Prince of Songkla University, Pattani Campus (Catalog Item 23)  

Curatorial Description
This modular baked clay artifact represents an authentic chatra-valee (tiered umbrella spire) crafted to crown the superstructure of a miniature terracotta votive stupa (Sathup Din Phao).  

The spire is constructed from 7 concentric, ring-shaped umbrella plates (chatra) engineered with raised central cylindrical sleeves. These interlocking components are stacked vertically along a central axis in decreasing diameter from the wide base plate to the narrow summit, forming an elegant graduated conical silhouette. The pinnacle terminates in a tall, elongated pointed lotus-bud finial (bua tum song sung).  

Archaeological & Spiritual Significance
Discovered within the ancient architectural complex of Ban Jaleh, this artifact showcases the specialized modular ceramic production techniques developed in the Yarang workshops during the height of the ancient maritime kingdom of Langkasuka.  

In Buddhist cosmology, the multi-tiered chatra-valee symbolizes the ascending celestial realms (heavens) and royal-spiritual sovereignty. Miniature stupas crowned with these multi-tiered spires were produced in vast numbers as devotional merit-making offerings and consecrated foundation deposits, reflecting the widespread presence of Mahayana Buddhism and the Cetiyavada school in early peninsular Southeast Asia.
