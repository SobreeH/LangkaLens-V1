Tripartite Stone Shiva Linga (ศิวลึงค์ศิลา)

- Period / Dating: c. 7th-8th Century CE (12th-13th Centuries Buddhist Era)
- Material: Carved sandstone
- Dimensions: Height: 58 cm | Width: 16 cm | Thickness: 16 cm
- Discovery Location: Western moat area, Ban Prawae Ancient Monument Group, Yarang District, Pattani Province (Found in 1977)
- Current Repository: Art and Cultural Center, Yala Rajabhat University

Curatorial Description:
This monolithic sandstone sculpture is a classic canonical (bap prapheni-niyom) Shiva linga sculpted into three equal vertical sections symbolizing the Hindu Trimurti:

- Rudra-bhaga (Top): A smooth, egg-shaped cylinder representing Lord Shiva, carved with a vertical central ridge (Brahmasutra) and curved lateral contour lines (Parsvasutra) extending toward the rear.
- Vishnu-bhaga (Middle): An octagonal prismatic shaft symbolizing Lord Vishnu, designed to sit within a sacred yoni pedestal.
- Brahma-bhaga (Base): A square prismatic block representing Lord Brahma, intended to be anchored into the sanctuary foundation.

The discovery of this artifact provides key material evidence of active Shaivite Hindu worship and syncretic religious culture flourishing alongside Buddhism within the ancient maritime entrepot of Yarang (Langkasuka).
