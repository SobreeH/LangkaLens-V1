Standing Stone Buddha in Vitarka Mudra (พระพุทธรูปศิลายืน ปางแสดงธรรม)

- Period / Dating: 13th-14th centuries Buddhist Era (c. 8th-9th centuries CE)
- Material: Carved stone (Sandstone)
- Dimensions: Height: 60 cm | Width: 16 cm
- Provenance: Ban Kampong Baru (present-day Ban Jaleh), Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province
- Current Repository: Unknown / Unrecorded

Curatorial Description:
This monolithic stone sculpture depicts a standing Buddha in a rigid upright frontal posture (samabhanga) resting atop an open double-lotus pedestal (bua khwam bua ngai). Both hands are raised symmetrically to perform the Vitarka Mudra—the gesture of teaching, debate, and intellectual exposition. The Buddha is draped in a diaphanous, sheer monastic robe (civara) covering both shoulders (hom khlum) that clings tightly to the body contours. The frontal hem of the mantle forms a broad U-shaped curve extending down to the shins, revealing an inner lower garment that falls to the ankles.

Facial and anatomical treatments reflect distinct regional modeling, characterized by a flat facial plane, downcast meditative eyes, wide lips, bold spiral hair curls, and a rounded, cylindrical ushnisha (cranial protuberance).

While the image shares stylistic affinities with Southern peninsular sculptures from Wat Kaeo and Ban U-thae (Surat Thani) as well as Central Thai Dvaravati traditions, it demonstrates a distinctive innovation unique to the ancient Yarang workshops: both hands are carved resting directly against the lower abdomen at navel level, firmly attached to the torso rather than raised independently at chest or waist height. This piece stands as vital physical evidence of the refined local craftsmanship and Mahayana Buddhist devotional networks flourishing in the ancient maritime kingdom of Langkasuka.
