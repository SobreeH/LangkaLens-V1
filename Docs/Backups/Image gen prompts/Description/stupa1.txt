Terracotta Miniature Stupa with Inverted-Bowl Dome

- Dating / Period: 12th-14th centuries Buddhist Era (approx. 7th-9th centuries CE)
- Material: Fine-grained baked clay (Terracotta)
- Discovery Location: Ban Wat / Ban Jaleh archaeological complex, Yarang Ancient City, Pattani Province
- Description: A free-standing votive stupa (Sathup Din Phao) crafted as a sacred Buddhist offering. The structure rises from a circular stepped pedestal with a distinct recessed waist (thong mai). The central bell dome is sculpted into an inverted-bowl form (ong rakhang song o khwam). Rising directly from the dome is an integrated conical spire formed by concentric horizontal ring ridges (plong chanai), which tapers steadily toward a pointed lotus-bud finial (bua tum). Votive objects of this type were buried within brick sanctuary foundations to generate religious merit.
